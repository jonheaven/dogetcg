# What Genesis 111 takes from other TCGs

## Selection

TCGplayer's Q3 2026 marketplace sales put Magic, Pokémon, One Piece, and Yu-Gi-Oh! in the first four positions by dollar volume, with Riftbound fifth and Lorcana sixth. That measures one marketplace's sales. Flesh and Blood, Digimon, and Netrunner round out the mechanics comparison because each explores a different design problem.

Publisher records also show active tournament scenes for Magic, Pokémon, Yu-Gi-Oh!, and One Piece. Null Signal Games reported 361 main-event players at its 2025 Netrunner World Championship. Tournament entries and marketplace sales capture different parts of a game's audience, so this study keeps them as separate signals.

| Study | Pattern in the source game | Genesis 111 decision |
| Pokémon | Recognizable characters, Trainer cards and a prize race. | Put Kabosu and Kitsunebi on opposite starter boxes. Give support cards their own identities. Signal is visible and requires a board action. |
| Magic | Mana curve, color identities and deckbuilding restrictions. | Six factions with meaningful weaknesses. Automatic Charge means every deck slot can be a Doge, Tactic, or Item. |
| One Piece | Dedicated DON!! supply and a face-up leader. | Reliable resources and a recognizable face for each starter. The mascot stays in the deck and can be drawn, answered or defeated. |
| Yu-Gi-Oh! | Distinct monster, spell and trap roles. | Use Doges, one-shot Tactics and lasting Items. Resolve plays immediately for a first tabletop version. |
| Disney Lorcana | Characters can be spent to advance a visible scoring race. | Secure uses a Doge's action. Its lane has to be open, so opponents can contest the score by deploying or rerouting. |
| Riftbound | Board positions are meaningful scoring locations. | Three lanes support blocking, flank pressure and repositioning. Securing costs an action. Open lanes also offer a different choice: direct damage. |
| Flesh and Blood | A card can serve more than one purpose and resource decisions have opportunity costs. | Move that tradeoff onto the board. Every ready Doge you spend on Signal or movement is one less attacker. |
| Digimon | A shared memory gauge makes the pace of play part of the strategy. | Keep resource tracking independent, with an increasing public Charge cap. Save tempo decisions for deployment, attack order and movement. |
| Netrunner | Position and distinct roles tell the story through rules. | Factions have asymmetric playstyles inside a shared turn structure. A payment courier plays differently from a wallet guardian. |

## What makes the card stack work

- Six faction pools contain ten Doges, four Tactics and two Items each. The shared pool adds nine Doges and six Tactics. That yields 69 Doges, 30 Tactics and 12 Items across 111 unique definitions.
- Every faction has three or more Doges at cost 1 or 2, a cheap interaction or support Tactic, and a costly payoff. All six starter lists have 30 cards, six Neutrals, early Doges, and legal copy counts.
- Cheap cards have clean jobs. Higher-rarity cards get stronger identities or conditional effects, with no effect from cosmetic finish. All 111 are available to build with in tabletop tests.
- For first tests, alternate starting player. Record turn count, first player, winner, path to victory, first useful action, unused Charge, and which cards felt mandatory. Review after ten games of the starter matchup and then play every faction against both starter factions. Flag repeated non-games, heavy first-player skew, and modes with no practical counterplay. The matchup and balance targets remain open playtest questions.

## The Dogecoin world on the cards

Ironclad turns nodes, confirmations, and cold storage into checkpoints, lanterns and vaults. Outriders wear merchant scarves and race payment slips between stalls. Ghostline moves letters through relays and changes routes through the night market. Ashbound recovers spent outputs and discarded inscriptions in the kiln district. Runekeepers stamp Doginals in the print quarter. Wildpack turns tipping and the community's goodwill into a pack strategy.

The set gives small characters room to be memorable. Faucet Pup catches its first coin, Genesis Miner works by a headlamp, Paper Wallet Shibe wears a straw cape, and the Multisig Brothers each carry a key. The heavier silhouettes belong to Cold Storage Doge, BurneÐ Custodian and Thunder General. Items get the same care: the Paper Wallet is a red-sealed keepsake, the Relay Beacon is a lantern tower, and the Dogenal Press has its own die and ink tray.

## Relationship to the earlier handoff

The repository starter provided useful deck rules, the three-lane Core-and-Signal premise, card-count targets, and a home for eventual integration with the existing Doginals stack. This tabletop design is a proposed rules branch. It puts one Doge on each side of each lane, allows interleaving plays and Doge actions, uses no reaction window, and uses the Japanese print direction. The named starter artwork is concept art. On-card prototype stats are the print files' current values. The prior repo, art manifest and protocol contract remain separate source material. Card IDs here use a playtest prefix so they cannot be confused with prior art recipe IDs.
